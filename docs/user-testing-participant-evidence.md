# MealMatch Anonymised Participant Evidence Appendix

## Purpose and handling

This appendix preserves the supplied participant-level observations used to
produce `user-testing-combined-results.md`. The wording has been normalised for
clarity and spelling, but missing measurements have not been invented. It is an
observer-note record, not a verbatim interview transcript. Participant codes do
not contain names or contact details.

## Integrated usability evidence

### P01

- The main call to action made entry easy and the purpose was clear.
- Photograph analysis took about 30 seconds and felt too slow. Three incorrect
  suggestions were removed, three ingredients were missed and some quantities
  were wrong. Photograph upload/review ease was `7/10`.
- Checking omissions felt annoying. The participant wanted direct quantity
  controls and units such as jar, box, piece and carton.
- Confirmation was considered important for pantry and shopping accuracy.
- Receipt abbreviations such as `artisan BGT` and repeated shrimp lines were
  handled poorly. The participant did not trust photo/receipt output without
  human review.
- Categories needed finer fruit, vegetable and meat groupings and clearer
  icons.
- Recommendations were good; pantry availability, cravings/cuisine, expiry
  priority and substitutions mattered.
- Hands-free help was useful when hands were occupied, but voice controls
  competed with progress/time. “Next” did not consistently advance the visible
  step while “next step” did.
- The AI recipe input looked pre-filled. The participant preferred several
  concepts before choosing a full recipe.
- Assistant-created recipes should become normal recipe cards and conversation
  history should last longer.
- Requested `Other` dietary preference and logo-to-Home navigation.
- Often forgot food and discarded it; expected pantry visibility and
  expiry-aware recipes to help with waste and decision effort.

### P02

- Entry was straightforward and the pantry/recommendation purpose was clear.
- Photograph analysis took 15–20 seconds and was acceptable, but needed progress
  feedback. Two partly hidden items were missed and packaged quantities were
  inaccurate. Ease was `8/10`.
- Wanted in-card editing, quantity `+`/`−` controls and units including bottle,
  packet, grams, pieces and cartons. Verification was important for overlapping
  objects.
- Receipt entry was convenient, but `CHKN BRST` was not interpreted correctly.
  The participant expected receipt entry after shopping and photograph entry
  when checking the fridge.
- Wanted fridge/freezer/pantry locations and easier expiry editing.
- Recommendations were useful; zero-missing-ingredient recipes, a “You already
  have everything” label, time and difficulty mattered. Some swaps felt unusual.
- Wanted a persistent timer. Hands-free control was useful around raw food, but
  “next” was inconsistent.
- Preferred three AI recipe concepts and wanted favourites support.
- Wanted the cooking assistant to know the selected recipe and retain history
  during cooking.
- Forgotten vegetables were a waste problem; expiry reminders plus recipes
  could help.

### P03

- Onboarding and the pantry-to-recipe concept were clear, but receipt scanning
  was not initially noticed.
- Photograph analysis took 25–30 seconds and felt slightly slow. Small garlic
  and chilli were missed and spinach quantity was incorrect. Ease was `7/10`.
- Wanted progress feedback, manual quantity entry, an adjacent unit field and
  learning from corrections. Verification was necessary but excessive
  correction could remove the convenience advantage.
- Preferred receipt entry. Abbreviations were unclear and duplicates were
  combined incorrectly; wanted grouped quantities and prices removed.
- Wanted stronger expiry colour and finer food categories.
- Recommendations were good but should rank pantry match first, offer a `100%`
  match filter and allow exclusion of an owned ingredient.
- Liked cooking progress, but wanted a smaller voice area and simultaneous
  current instruction, timer and next-step visibility. “Next step” worked and
  “repeat that” was expected to repeat the instruction.
- Wanted 3–5 AI concepts showing name, image, time and pantry match.
- Preferred the flexible assistant and wanted its history retained for the
  cooking session.
- Often forgot sauces and vegetables; considered expiry-based recommendations
  a strong feature.

### P04

- The homepage was attractive but the pantry-versus-recipe purpose was initially
  ambiguous; a one-line description was suggested.
- Photograph analysis took 10–15 seconds and felt fast. Large foods were found,
  but packaged products could be labelled too generally. Two items were changed.
  Ease was `9/10`.
- Preferred quantity steppers and quick presets. Verification was useful, and
  low-confidence suggestions should be distinguished.
- Receipt entry was less personally useful because receipts were not usually
  retained; digital receipts could be more relevant. Abbreviations remained a
  problem.
- Wanted pantry search and sorting by expiry, category and recency.
- Recommendations were very good; cooking time mattered more than cuisine and
  substitutions prevented abandoning a recipe over one missing item.
- Liked progress and large instructions. Unlike several others, did not want the
  hands-free area reduced and wanted a stronger microphone indicator.
- Preferred one complete AI recipe plus regeneration because multiple choices
  could recreate decision fatigue.
- Wanted the assistant as a floating cooking control but did not need permanent
  history.
- Rarely wasted food; expected more value for meal decisions than waste
  reduction.

### P05

- The main call to action was clear even though some homepage copy was skipped.
- Photograph analysis took about 35 seconds, felt slow and initially appeared
  frozen. Four items were missed, transparent packaging performed poorly and
  most quantities were wrong. Ease was `6/10`.
- Wanted progress feedback and a compact list/spreadsheet editor for name,
  quantity, unit and expiry. Confirmation was useful only if faster than manual
  entry.
- Preferred receipt entry, but abbreviations and repeated quantities failed.
  Uncertain lines should be surfaced rather than silently guessed.
- Wanted bulk pantry editing, a `used up` action and storage locations.
- Recommendations were relevant but basic; wanted both best matches and novel
  ideas, expiry priority, cuisine and craving input.
- Voice advancement was difficult; wanted “next”, “go back”, “repeat”, “how
  long?” and “start timer”. The feature remained promising if recognition
  improved.
- AI recipe input was unclear; wanted examples and several concepts first.
- Wanted assistant-generated recipes saved as normal cards and strong
  conversation continuity.
- Frequently discarded vegetables, herbs and dairy every one or two weeks;
  considered expiry tracking especially important.

### P06

- Understood the application quickly and liked the short onboarding.
- Photograph analysis took about 20 seconds and felt acceptable. One occluded
  item was missed and egg quantity was inaccurate. Ease was `9/10`.
- Verification increased trust. Preferred typing quantity and automatic
  ingredient-specific unit suggestions.
- Receipt entry was useful after shopping; duplicate lines should be summed and
  supermarket abbreviations remained a risk.
- Wanted custom categories and restrained use of ingredient icons.
- Recommendations were relevant; wanted `use soon` emphasis and missing-item
  counts. Exact pantry match was not always more important than recipe quality.
- Hands-free was the favourite feature; “next step” worked. Wanted voice timers
  and screen-wake behaviour.
- Preferred one full AI recipe with pre-generation controls for time, cuisine
  and difficulty.
- Wanted automatic pantry context. Session history was useful but unnecessary
  after completing the recipe.
- Requested vegetarian, pescatarian, `Other` and custom-allergy settings.
- Often forgot opened cream and sauces; valued both pantry awareness and
  decision support.

### P07

- Entry was easy, but many features could make navigation confusing; wanted
  clearer separation of Pantry, Recipes and Cooking.
- Photograph analysis took about 25 seconds and felt reasonable. Packaged foods
  were difficult, one item was duplicated and two were missed. Ease was
  `7.5/10`.
- Wanted multi-select deletion, mobile swipe-to-delete and simpler quantity
  editing. Verification protected downstream recommendations.
- Receipt interpretation felt less reliable; wanted the original line shown
  beside the interpreted ingredient.
- Wanted expiry notifications, automatic consumption after cooking and movement
  of depleted items to shopping.
- Wanted recommendations to learn from likes/dislikes and provide quick,
  healthy, high-protein and cuisine filters.
- Cooking required too much scrolling; wanted a fixed current step and
  collapsible voice controls. The participant naturally said “next”.
- Wanted three AI concepts formatted as normal recipe cards.
- Wanted cooking-assistant questions grounded in the current step.
- Frequently left remainders after one recipe; suggested a `Use My Leftovers`
  feature.

### P08

- The call to action and design made the application feel simple and the purpose
  was immediately clear.
- Photograph analysis took 12–15 seconds and felt fast. Three quantities were
  inaccurate and one small corner packet was missed. Ease was `9/10`.
- Checking was not annoying; wanted compact cards, quantity steppers and common
  units. Expected AI errors and valued verification.
- Preferred receipt entry for a whole shopping trip; wanted multiple images for
  long receipts and aggregated duplicate quantities.
- Wanted purchase dates and editable category-based expiry estimates; fruit and
  vegetables should be separate.
- Recommendations were relevant but needed stronger craving/mood
  personalisation and an availability-to-discovery control.
- Wanted timer and current step visible together and an audible recognition cue.
- Preferred several AI choices and delayed full instructions until selection.
- Wanted the assistant to modify and save the recipe card.
- Requested disliked-food preferences.
- Discarded food a few times per month through forgetting and duplicate buying;
  valued pantry visibility and expiry-prioritised recipes.

### P09

- The first screen was understandable, but the feature order was unclear; wanted
  a short Add → Check → Find → Cook tutorial.
- Photograph analysis took 30–40 seconds and felt too slow. Three ingredients
  were correct, two incorrect and three missed. Ease was `5/10`.
- Wanted barcode entry for packaged goods alongside photos for produce, receipts
  after shopping and manual backup. Quantity and unit controls remained needed.
- Receipt accuracy was better for this participant, although abbreviations and
  uncertainty indicators still mattered.
- Wanted grocery-list integration and movement of zero-quantity items.
- Recommendations were decent but needed stronger taste learning. Preferred an
  80% match in a liked cuisine over a disliked exact match.
- Text-only cooking was less engaging; suggested media for difficult techniques.
  Hands-free remained useful.
- Wanted AI recipes labelled and supported by ratings; preferred established
  recipes where possible.
- The assistant was useful for short substitution/time questions but did not
  need long-term history.
- Rarely forgot pantry contents; expected more value for recipe discovery and
  grocery integration than waste reduction.

### P10

- Liked the design but explored before understanding the complete workflow. The
  non-navigating logo was noticed immediately.
- Photograph analysis took 20–25 seconds and was acceptable, though expected to
  become faster. Similar leafy vegetables were confused and quantities were
  inaccurate. Ease was `8/10`.
- Wanted steppers, units and an `approximately` option. Verification made pantry
  changes transparent.
- Expected receipt entry to become the default. Wanted retailer correction
  memory and duplicate aggregation.
- Wanted consumption tracking, opened/unopened state and expanded categories.
- Wanted rank explanations such as pantry items used, expiry urgency and missing
  count, without one expiring ingredient dominating every result.
- Liked progress/timer, wanted a smaller hands-free area and broader natural
  commands. “Next” was inconsistent.
- Wanted 3–4 AI concepts as normal saveable/rateable cards.
- Wanted the assistant connected to pantry, recipe and step, with recipe changes
  applied directly and history retained for the recipe duration.
- Requested `Other`, disliked-food and metric-unit preferences.
- Frequently bought duplicates and discarded vegetables, yoghurt and sauces;
  valued pantry visibility and expiry-prioritised recipes.

## Post-iteration cohort-level evidence

The same participant cohort tested the revised prototype again. The supplied
follow-up notes report that all attempted natural verbal indications of wanting
to move to the next cooking step advanced the visible recipe card, that AI recipe
generation felt quicker, and that the three retained photo-analysis timings were
`5`, `4` and `5` seconds.

These observations were supplied at cohort level rather than by anonymous
participant ID. They are therefore not assigned retrospectively to `P01`–`P10`.
The voice re-test attempt count and recipe-generation timestamps were not
retained. The photo runs have a mean of `4.67` seconds and median of `5` seconds;
compared with the earlier approximate participant-observed median of `23.75`
seconds, the median is `18.75` seconds (`78.9%`) lower. Because the images and
conditions differed, this is descriptive evidence of iteration rather than a
controlled paired comparison.

After design iteration 3, the same cohort re-tested the AI recipe generator,
swaps and recipe browsing. The supplied notes report, at cohort level, that the
median recipe-generation time was lower, that generated recipes matched the
request and used pantry ingredients better, that swaps were rated as more
useful, and that usability was higher because AI idea cards stayed and the
recipe list kept its scroll position. The underlying timings and ratings were
not supplied and are not reconstructed.
